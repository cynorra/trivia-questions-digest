## Tricks to remember the order

Nobody memorizes 47 names in one go. Learn them in chunks tied to an era you already know.

- **The Founders (1–5):** Washington, Adams, Jefferson, Madison, Monroe. Four of the five were Virginians; Adams, from Massachusetts, is the odd one out, and he comes second.
- **Sons and Jacksonians (6–11):** John Quincy Adams (John Adams's son), Jackson, Van Buren, Harrison, Tyler, Polk. "Tippecanoe and Tyler too," Harrison's campaign slogan, ties 9 and 10 together.
- **Road to the Civil War (12–15):** Taylor, Fillmore, Pierce, Buchanan. Four short presidencies in a row before Lincoln.
- **Civil War and Reconstruction (16–19):** Lincoln, Johnson, Grant, Hayes.
- **The Gilded Age (20–25):** Garfield, Arthur, Cleveland, Harrison, Cleveland, McKinley. Cleveland sandwiches Benjamin Harrison, the grandson of the 9th president.
- **The Progressive era and the 1920s (26–31):** the two Roosevelts are separated by a generation; between Teddy and Hoover come Taft, Wilson, Harding and Coolidge.
- **Depression to the Cold War (32–38):** FDR, Truman, Eisenhower, Kennedy, Johnson, Nixon, Ford.
- **Living memory (39–47):** Carter, Reagan, Bush, Clinton, Bush, Obama, Trump, Biden, Trump.

Four of the eight presidents who died in office were assassinated: Lincoln, Garfield, McKinley and Kennedy. The 22nd Amendment, ratified in 1951 after Roosevelt's four elections, limits presidents to two elected terms.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many presidents has the United States had?

As of 2026, 45 people have served in 47 presidencies. Grover Cleveland and Donald Trump each served two non-consecutive terms and are counted twice.

### Who was the 16th president?

Abraham Lincoln, who served from 1861 until his assassination in April 1865.

### Which presidents served non-consecutive terms?

Grover Cleveland, the 22nd and 24th president, and Donald Trump, the 45th and 47th.

### What's the easiest way to memorize the presidents in order?

Learn them in short groups tied to eras, such as the five Founders or the Civil War presidents, and practice one group a day.
