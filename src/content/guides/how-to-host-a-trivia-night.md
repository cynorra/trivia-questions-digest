Plan for about two hours including breaks, and pick at least one round that suits every age and interest in the room.</p></div>

Hosting a trivia night is mostly about rhythm. The questions matter, but what people remember is whether the evening moved: no long silences, no arguments about what the host said, and a close finish. This plan works for a living room, an office party or a bar, and you can lift every question you need from our [trivia categories](/general-knowledge-trivia/).

## Before the night: decide the format

Settle these four things first, because everything else follows from them.

For a smaller family gathering, four rounds of eight questions is plenty. For a large bar crowd, keep six rounds but make teams no bigger than six; bigger teams get loud and stop listening.

## Choose rounds people actually enjoy

The most common mistake is six rounds of the host's favorite subjects. Mix it up:

1. **Warm-up: general knowledge.** Easy questions that everyone can answer. Our [general knowledge trivia](/general-knowledge-trivia/) works well here.
2. **A history or science round.** Try [history trivia](/history-trivia/) or [science trivia](/science-trivia/).
3. **Pop culture.** [Movie and TV trivia](/movie-trivia/) or [music trivia](/music-trivia/). Add short audio clips for a "name that tune" twist.
4. **A themed round.** Match the season: [Christmas](/christmas-trivia/), [Thanksgiving](/thanksgiving-trivia/) or [Halloween](/halloween-trivia/).
5. **A picture or wildcard round.** Celebrity baby photos, logos, or a "name the year" round.
6. **The hard round.** Finish with our [trivia questions for adults](/trivia-questions-for-adults/), worth double points to keep everyone in the race.

More formats, from "connections" rounds to sound rounds, are in our list of [trivia round ideas](/guides/trivia-round-ideas/).

## Write or pick questions with one clear answer

Every question should have exactly one answer that a reasonable person would accept. "What's the biggest country?" is a bad question (by area? by population?). "Which country is the largest by area?" is a good one. Before the night, read every question out loud once: anything you stumble over, the room will misunderstand.

If you write your own, our guide on [how to write trivia questions](/guides/how-to-write-trivia-questions/) explains how to choose wrong options and avoid trick questions that annoy people.

## Running the room

- **Read each question twice**, then the options, then the question once more. People mishear in loud rooms.
- **Give a time warning** ("ten seconds") before moving on.
- **Collect answer sheets at the end of each round**, not each question. It's faster and stops teams changing answers.
- **Read the answers with a one-line explanation.** Every question on this site comes with one. It turns "the answer was B" into a moment people talk about.
- **Post the scores between rounds.** A visible leaderboard is the single best way to keep energy up.

## Scoring and tiebreakers

Give one point per correct answer and double the final round. Decide before you start whether you accept close answers (spelling mistakes, last names only). Write the rule on the board so no one argues later.

Always have a tiebreaker ready: a number question where the closest answer wins, such as "How many gifts are given in 'The Twelve Days of Christmas'?" (364) or "How many laps make up the Indianapolis 500?" (200). Our guide to [trivia night rules and scoring](/guides/trivia-night-rules-and-scoring/) covers bonus rounds, jokers and how to handle disputes.

## Prizes and timing

Prizes don't need to be expensive. A box of chocolates, a homemade trophy or bragging rights on a framed certificate works. A prize for last place keeps the bottom teams laughing instead of leaving.

## A quick checklist

- Questions printed, with answers on a separate sheet
- Answer sheets and pens for every team
- A visible scoreboard (whiteboard or a shared screen)
- A timer
- A tiebreaker question and a backup
- A microphone if more than about 30 people are coming

## Frequently asked questions

### How many questions should a trivia night have?

Around 60 is typical: six rounds of ten. For a short office or family game, 30 to 40 questions over three or four rounds is enough.

### How long does a trivia night last?

About two hours, including a break and the prize-giving. Each round of ten questions takes 10 to 15 minutes with marking.

### What's a good team size for a pub quiz?

Four to six players per team. With more than six, some players stop contributing and the table gets too loud to hear the host.
