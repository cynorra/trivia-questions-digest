Thirteen states touch Canada and four touch Mexico, and no state touches both. Those two borders are among the quickest things to learn on the US map, and they help you place the states around them. The easiest way to learn them is to walk each line from west to east, so that is how this guide runs.

## Walking the Canadian border, west to east

**Alaska** starts the line, bordering the Yukon and British Columbia by land. It has the longest land border with Canada of any state.

**Washington** meets British Columbia, by land and water. **Idaho** reaches British Columbia too, by land via its panhandle. **Montana** is next, touching British Columbia, Alberta and Saskatchewan along its northern edge, and **North Dakota** borders Saskatchewan and Manitoba.

Between Washington and Minnesota, much of the border follows the 49th parallel, a straight line of latitude set by treaties in 1818 and 1846. That's why the border looks ruler-drawn across the plains.

**Minnesota** borders Manitoba and Ontario, by land and water. It also has the Northwest Angle, a small piece of land north of the 49th parallel that can be reached by land only through Canada. It's the northernmost point of the lower 48 states.

Then the border goes into the water. **Michigan** borders Ontario through the Great Lakes and rivers, and **Ohio** and **Pennsylvania** both meet Ontario in the middle of Lake Erie. These three are the ones people forget, because there is no line of land to see. **New York** borders Ontario and Quebec by water and land.

The line then returns to land in New England: **Vermont** and **New Hampshire** both border Quebec, and **Maine** borders Quebec and New Brunswick, by land and water.

That is thirteen states: Alaska, Washington, Idaho, Montana, North Dakota, Minnesota, Michigan, Ohio, Pennsylvania, New York, Vermont, New Hampshire and Maine. The US–Canada border is the longest international border in the world.

## Walking the Mexican border, west to east

Only four states, and the order is easy.

- **California** borders Baja California. It is the shortest of the four and meets the Pacific south of San Diego.
- **Arizona** borders Baja California and Sonora, mostly by land.
- **New Mexico** borders Chihuahua and Sonora, and includes the southwestern "bootheel."
- **Texas** borders Chihuahua, Coahuila, Nuevo León and Tamaulipas. It is the longest of the four, following the Rio Grande from El Paso all the way to the Gulf of Mexico. West of El Paso, the border runs over land through New Mexico, Arizona and California.
