"Tell us a fun fact about yourself" is the most common icebreaker there is, and it makes almost everyone freeze. The trick is that a fun fact doesn't need to be impressive. It needs to be specific. "I like hiking" is a hobby; "I once hiked 20 miles in a day by taking a wrong turn" is a fun fact.

## How to find your fun fact

Ask yourself these questions and write down anything that makes you smile:

1. **Firsts and onlys:** Is there something you were the first or only person in your family, class or team to do?
2. **Places:** Where have you lived, worked or traveled that people wouldn't guess?
3. **Odd skills:** What can you do that most people can't, however small?
4. **Near-misses:** Have you almost met, been mistaken for or crossed paths with someone famous?
5. **Numbers:** Is there a number in your life that sounds surprising: siblings, moves, countries, a collection?
6. **Changes of direction:** Did you once plan to do something completely different?

Pick the answer that would make someone ask "How?" or "Why?" That follow-up question is what turns an icebreaker into a conversation.

## 50 example fun facts about yourself

Use these as patterns. Swap in your own details; a true, specific fact beats a borrowed one.

### For work and team meetings

1. I've worked in three different industries before this one.
2. I can type over 100 words a minute.
3. My first job was in a [ice cream shop / summer camp / call center].
4. I've lived in [number] cities and never in the one I was born in.
5. I once gave a presentation to a room of 500 people.
6. I've never had a cup of coffee.
7. I learned to code from library books.
8. I answer every email the same day, even on vacation. (Ask your manager whether that's a fun fact.)
9. I keep a spreadsheet of every book I've read since college.
10. I've visited every state on the East Coast.

### Skills and talents

11. I can solve a Rubik's Cube.
12. I can name every US president in order.
13. I can whistle with two fingers loud enough to stop a taxi.
14. I can juggle three balls.
15. I can say "hello" in ten languages.
16. I'm ambidextrous.
17. I can draw a convincing map of the world from memory.
18. I can recite the first 30 digits of pi.
19. I was a competitive [swimmer / chess player / speller] as a kid.
20. I can cook a full meal without a recipe.

### Travel and places

21. I've been to [number] countries.
22. I was born on a holiday.
23. I've seen the northern lights.
24. I've lived on an island.
25. I've slept in an airport more than once.
26. I've been to the same restaurant on three continents.
27. I grew up in a town of fewer than 1,000 people.
28. I've crossed the equator by boat.
29. I've driven across the whole country.
30. I've climbed a mountain over 10,000 feet.

### Funny and unusual

31. I've never seen [a famous film everyone has seen].
32. I was in a TV commercial as a child.
33. I've been mistaken for a celebrity.
34. I share a birthday with [a famous person].
35. I've never broken a bone.
36. I have a twin, or I've been mistaken for someone's twin.
37. I once won a pie-eating contest.
38. I can't whistle at all.
39. I named my car.
40. I've kept the same houseplant alive for ten years.

### Interesting and personal

41. I'm the youngest of six.
42. I was the first person in my family to go to college.
43. I learned to ride a bike as an adult.
44. I've read every book by one author.
45. I've run a marathon.
46. I can trace my family back eight generations.
47. I've volunteered at a [zoo / food bank / hospital].
48. I've written a song, even if nobody has heard it.
49. I've been on a game show, or tried out for one.
50. I once met [someone well known] by accident.

## Three interesting facts and two truths and a lie

Some icebreakers ask for **three interesting facts**. Choose three from different areas: one about work or school, one about a place, one skill or quirk. Variety makes you memorable.

For **two truths and a lie**, the best lie is believable and the best truth sounds made up. Pair one ordinary true fact, one surprising true fact and one plausible lie. Our list of [popular "facts" that are wrong](/guides/common-trivia-myths/) shows how convincing a false statement can be.

## Fun facts for kids and students

At school, keep facts simple: a pet's name, a favorite book, a place you've been, something you can do. Teachers often use "fun fact Friday" to let students share one each week.

## When you're asked for a general fun fact

Sometimes "fun fact" means a fact about the world, not about you. Then pick something short and surprising, like "octopuses have three hearts." Our [fun facts](/fun-facts/) page has dozens, each with a source, and a new one every day.

## If you can't think of one

Everyone has one: a first, an odd skill, a surprising number or a near-miss with someone famous. Even "I've never seen Star Wars" gets a reaction. For a work meeting, keep it light and work-safe, such as a first job, a skill or a place you've lived, and avoid anything too personal, political or that could embarrass someone else.
