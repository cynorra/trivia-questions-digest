"What is the capital of…?" usually has one answer. For a handful of countries it doesn't, because the constitution names one city while the government sits in another, or because the job is deliberately shared. The reasons are interesting history, and they fall into three groups, so this guide is organized by reason rather than alphabetically. It ends with the countries that moved their capital and how our quiz handles the awkward cases.

## Reason 1: a compromise between rival cities

**South Africa** has three capitals. The executive sits in Pretoria, parliament in Cape Town, and the Supreme Court of Appeal in Bloemfontein. The arrangement dates back to 1910, when four colonies united: rather than choose one city, the new country shared the roles among three of them.

**Bolivia** has two. Sucre is the constitutional capital, and the government sits in La Paz. Both answers appear in references, which is why quizzes often skip Bolivia.

## Reason 2: the constitution says one thing, the government does another

**Netherlands.** Amsterdam is the capital by the constitution, although parliament and government are in The Hague.

**Benin.** Porto-Novo is the official capital; Cotonou is the seat of government.

**Côte d'Ivoire.** Yamoussoukro is the official capital; Abidjan is the economic center.

**Sri Lanka.** Parliament is in Sri Jayawardenepura Kotte; Colombo is the commercial capital.

**Eswatini.** Mbabane is the administrative capital; Lobamba is the royal and legislative capital.

## Reason 3: federal offices moved out of the big city

**Malaysia.** Kuala Lumpur is the capital, but many federal offices moved to the planned city of Putrajaya.

## Countries that moved their capital outright

Usually to place the capital more centrally, to relieve a crowded coastal city, or to build a planned city as a national symbol:

- **Brazil:** from Rio de Janeiro to Brasília, a planned city, in 1960.
- **Nigeria:** from Lagos to Abuja in 1991.
- **Myanmar:** from Yangon to Naypyidaw in the mid-2000s.
- **Kazakhstan:** from Almaty to Akmola, renamed Astana, in 1997. The city was called Nur-Sultan from 2019 to 2022.
- **Tanzania:** Dodoma became the official capital, though Dar es Salaam remains the largest city.
- **Burundi:** Gitega became the political capital in 2019; Bujumbura is the largest city.

Indonesia has also been building a new capital, Nusantara, on the island of Borneo, to take over from Jakarta. Check current news for its status, since the move is happening in stages.

## Special cases

- **Switzerland** has no capital in law. Bern is officially the "federal city," where the government sits.
- **Nauru** has no official capital; government offices are in the district of Yaren.
- **Israel and Palestine:** the status of Jerusalem is disputed internationally. Our quiz leaves both out of capital questions rather than take a side.

## How our capitals quiz handles these

The [world capitals quiz](/world-capitals-quiz/) follows one rule: if a single answer would be misleading, the country isn't asked. That excludes Bolivia, South Africa, Sri Lanka, Eswatini, Nauru, Israel and Palestine. For countries with a clear official capital but a well-known second city, such as the Netherlands or Côte d'Ivoire, the quiz asks for the official capital and shows a note with the answer.
