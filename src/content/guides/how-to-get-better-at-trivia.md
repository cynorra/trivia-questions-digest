<div class="key-takeaway"><p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> Strong trivia players aren't walking encyclopedias. They learn a set of anchor facts in the most common categories, practice a little every day, read the explanation behind every answer they miss, and use logic to eliminate wrong options. Ten minutes a day, such as one daily quiz, adds up quickly.</p></div>

The good news about trivia is that the same topics come up again and again: presidents, planets, capitals, famous films, Bible stories, sports firsts. You don't need to know everything, just the things quizzes tend to ask, and a way to reason when you're not sure.

## 1. Learn the anchor lists

A few lists answer a surprising share of questions. Learn them once and they pay off for years:

- The [US presidents in order](/guides/us-presidents-in-order/)
- The [planets in order](/guides/planets-in-order/) and their key facts
- The major dates of [World War II](/guides/world-war-2-timeline/)
- The [books of the Bible](/guides/books-of-the-bible-in-order/), if your quizzes include Bible rounds
- The continents and oceans, and the capitals of the largest countries

## 2. Know the common categories

Most quizzes draw on the same dozen subjects: general knowledge, history, science, geography, sports, film and TV, music, food, and holidays. Spend your study time where the questions are. Our category pages, from [general knowledge](/general-knowledge-trivia/) to [music](/music-trivia/), cover each one.

## 3. Practice a little every day

Short, regular practice beats long cramming. A daily quiz, such as [today's trivia quiz](/daily-trivia/), takes about five minutes and spreads questions across categories. Over a month that's 300 questions, each with an explanation.

## 4. Read the explanation, not just the answer

When you miss a question, the explanation is where the learning is. "Venus, not Mercury, is the hottest planet because its atmosphere traps heat" teaches you a reason, and reasons stick far better than isolated facts.

## 5. Use multiple-choice logic

When you're unsure, eliminate:

- **Remove the impossible.** If the question is about the 1960s, an option from the 1990s is out.
- **Watch for the obvious trap.** Quiz writers often include the answer most people believe, such as Sydney for Australia's capital. If an option seems too obvious, ask why it's there.
- **Prefer the specific.** Between two plausible options, the more precise one is often right.
- **Go with your first instinct** if you have a reason for it; change only when you remember something concrete.

## 6. Connect new facts to things you know

A fact attached to something you already know is easier to recall. Knowing Nixon was president during the Moon landing links two facts at once. Knowing Canberra was built as a compromise between Sydney and Melbourne makes the capital hard to forget.

## 7. Keep up with a few areas

Pop culture and sports change fastest. You don't need to follow everything, but skimming the year's big films, the Super Bowl result and the Oscar winners covers most "recent" questions.

## 8. Build a balanced team

At a pub quiz, the best team isn't four history buffs. It's a mix: someone for sports, someone for music and film, someone for science, someone for history. Talk through each question briefly and trust the person who knows the area.

## 9. Learn the myths

Some questions are built around popular misconceptions, and some quizzes get them wrong. Knowing the common ones, from goldfish memory to Napoleon's height, helps either way. See [15 popular facts that are wrong](/guides/common-trivia-myths/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How can I get better at trivia fast?

Learn a few high-value lists, such as the presidents and the planets, and play a short daily quiz. Read the explanation for every question you miss.

### What topics come up most in trivia?

General knowledge, history, science, geography, sports, film and TV, music, food and holidays. US presidents and the planets are especially common.

### Is it better to guess in trivia?

In most pub quizzes there's no penalty for a wrong answer, so always answer. Use elimination to improve your odds.

### How do pub quiz teams win?

By mixing members with different strengths, discussing each question briefly, and trusting whoever knows the subject best.
