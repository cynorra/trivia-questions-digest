Most look-alike flags differ in exactly one detail, and the detail is always one of four kinds: the order of the colors, the shade of one color, the proportions, or a small emblem. Many flags share colors on purpose. The pan-African colors (green, yellow and red), the pan-Arab colors (red, white, black and green) and the red, white and blue of many European tricolors each appear on dozens of flags, which is why a guess-the-flag quiz feels easy for the first twenty and hard for the rest.

The fix isn't to memorize every flag separately. It's to learn the confusable ones in pairs and remember only the difference. Here they are, sorted by the kind of difference.

## When only the order differs

**Poland and Indonesia** both have red and white halves. Poland is white on top and red below; Indonesia is the reverse.

**Ireland and Côte d'Ivoire** both have green, white and orange vertical stripes. Ireland starts with green at the flagpole; Côte d'Ivoire starts with orange.

**Serbia and Russia** share white, blue and red. Serbia's stripes run red, blue, white from the top, the reverse of Russia's.

## When only the shade differs

**Chad and Romania** have vertical blue, yellow and red stripes in the same order. Chad's blue is a slightly darker shade. In a quiz, think about the continent: Romania is in Europe, Chad in Africa.

**The Netherlands and Luxembourg** both have red, white and blue horizontal stripes. Luxembourg's lower stripe is a lighter, sky blue.

## When only the proportions differ

**Indonesia and Monaco** are both red over white, with the same colors. Monaco's flag is a little squarer; Indonesia's is longer.

## When only the emblem differs

**The pan-Slavic tricolors.** Russia, Slovakia, Slovenia and Serbia all use white, blue and red stripes. Russia's is plain; Slovakia, Slovenia and Serbia each add a coat of arms toward the flagpole.

**Australia and New Zealand.** Both show the British Union Jack in the corner and the stars of the Southern Cross. Australia's flag adds a large seven-pointed star beneath the Union Jack and uses white stars; New Zealand's has four red stars outlined in white.

**The pan-Arab flags.** Jordan, Palestine, Sudan, Kuwait and the United Arab Emirates use red, white, black and green. Look at the shape at the flagpole: a red triangle for Jordan (with a small white star) and Palestine, a green triangle for Sudan, a black trapezoid for Kuwait, and a red vertical band for the UAE.

## The Nordic crosses: five flags, five color schemes

Denmark, Norway, Sweden, Finland and Iceland all use a cross shifted toward the flagpole, so the colors are the whole test:

- **Denmark:** white cross on red.
- **Norway:** blue cross outlined in white, on red.
- **Sweden:** yellow cross on blue.
- **Finland:** blue cross on white.
- **Iceland:** red cross outlined in white, on blue.

## The one with no twin

Nepal's flag is the only national flag that isn't a rectangle. It's two stacked triangles, and you'll never confuse it with anything.

## How to practice look-alikes

1. Play the [flag quiz](/flag-quiz/) with a single continent selected. Wrong choices come from the same continent, so the look-alikes appear together.
2. When you miss a flag, find its twin in the "Every flag in the quiz" list on the same page and say the difference out loud.
3. Do a round of the [daily geography quiz](/daily-geography-quiz/), which includes three flags every day.
