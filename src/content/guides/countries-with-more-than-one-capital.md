"What is the capital of…?" usually has one answer. For a handful of countries it doesn't, because the constitution names one city while the government sits in another, or because the job is deliberately shared. These are the cases worth knowing, both for quizzes and because the reasons are interesting history.

South Africa's arrangement dates back to 1910, when four colonies united. Rather than choose one city, the new country shared the roles among three of them.

## Countries that moved their capital

Some countries built or chose a new capital, usually to place it more centrally or to move away from a crowded coastal city:

- **Brazil:** from Rio de Janeiro to Brasília, a planned city, in 1960.
- **Nigeria:** from Lagos to Abuja in 1991.
- **Myanmar:** from Yangon to Naypyidaw in the mid-2000s.
- **Kazakhstan:** from Almaty to Akmola, renamed Astana, in 1997. The city was called Nur-Sultan from 2019 to 2022.
- **Tanzania:** Dodoma became the official capital, though Dar es Salaam remains the largest city.
- **Burundi:** Gitega became the political capital in 2019; Bujumbura is the largest city.

Indonesia has also been building a new capital, Nusantara, on the island of Borneo, to take over from Jakarta. Check current news for its status, since the move is happening in stages.

## Special cases

- **Switzerland** has no capital in law. Bern is officially the "federal city," where the government sits.
- **Nauru** has no official capital; government offices are in the district of Yaren.
- **Israel and Palestine:** the status of Jerusalem is disputed internationally. Our quiz leaves both out of capital questions rather than take a side.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which country has three capitals?

South Africa: Pretoria (executive), Cape Town (legislative) and Bloemfontein (judicial).

### What is the capital of Bolivia?

Sucre is the constitutional capital, and La Paz is the seat of government. Both answers appear in references, which is why quizzes often skip Bolivia.

### What is the capital of the Netherlands?

Amsterdam, by the constitution, although the government and parliament are in The Hague.

### Why do countries move their capital?

Usually to place it more centrally, to relieve a crowded coastal city, or to build a planned city as a national symbol, as Brazil did with Brasília.
