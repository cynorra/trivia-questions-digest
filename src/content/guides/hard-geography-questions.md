The hardest geography questions aren't about obscure places. They are about familiar places where intuition is wrong: Reno is west of Los Angeles, Canada has the longest coastline, and Africa is far bigger than most maps make it look. These twenty questions are grouped by the reason people miss them, so each group teaches one correction you can use on the next question too. Try each one before reading the answer, and keep score: most people get fewer than half.

For a quiz version with forty more questions, multiple choice and explanations, try [geography trivia](/geography-trivia/).

## Wrong mental map: we picture the world as straight lines

**1. Which city is farther west: Reno, Nevada, or Los Angeles, California?**
Reno. California's coast curves southeast, so Los Angeles sits east of Reno even though it's on the Pacific.

**2. If you travel due south from Detroit, which country do you reach first?**
Canada. Windsor, Ontario, lies directly south of downtown Detroit, across the Detroit River.

**3. Which US state is closest to Africa?**
Maine. Its eastern tip is the closest point in the US to Africa, slightly closer than Florida or the Carolinas.

**4. Which is farther north: Rome or New York City?**
Rome, slightly. Rome is at about 41.9° north and New York City at about 40.7°. Europe feels farther south than it is because the Gulf Stream keeps it mild, and farther north on the map because of the next problem.

## Map distortion: the Mercator projection

The Mercator projection, used on many wall maps and online maps, makes places near the poles look huge.

**5. Which is larger by area: Greenland or Africa?**
Africa, by about fourteen times. On the common Mercator map Greenland looks similar in size.

**6. Which country has the longest coastline?**
Canada. Its Arctic islands give it more coastline than any other country.

**7. Which US state has the longest coastline?**
Alaska, longer than all the other states combined.

**8. Which is the largest country entirely in Europe?**
Ukraine. Russia is larger but spans Europe and Asia.

## Famous-city bias: the biggest city must be the capital

**9. What is the capital of Australia?**
Canberra. Sydney and Melbourne were rivals, and Canberra was built as a compromise between them.

**10. What is the capital of Canada?**
Ottawa, not Toronto.

**11. What is the capital of Turkey?**
Ankara, not Istanbul.

**12. Which country has three capital cities?**
South Africa: Pretoria (executive), Cape Town (legislative) and Bloemfontein (judicial).

**13. What is the capital of Myanmar?**
Naypyidaw, which replaced Yangon in the mid-2000s.

## "It depends how you measure"

**14. Which river is longer, the Nile or the Amazon?**
Most references say the Nile, but some measurements put the Amazon ahead. The answer depends on where each river's source is placed.

**15. What is the largest lake in the world?**
The Caspian Sea, which despite its name is a lake. The largest freshwater lake by area is Lake Superior.

**16. Which country has the most time zones, counting overseas territories?**
France, with 12 (13 counting its Antarctic claim), because of territories from the Caribbean to the Pacific. Russia has the most within one continuous landmass, with 11.

**17. Is Australia an island?**
It's usually called a continent rather than an island. That's why Greenland is listed as the world's largest island.

## Borders and the small print

**18. Which two countries share the longest border?**
Canada and the United States.

**19. Which three countries are completely surrounded by a single other country?**
Lesotho, inside South Africa, and San Marino and Vatican City, both inside Italy.

**20. Which ocean is the smallest?**
The Arctic Ocean.

## Fixing the habit

The [world map quiz](/world-map-quiz/) uses the Equal Earth projection, which keeps countries' relative sizes accurate, so practicing on it slowly corrects the Mercator habit. These questions also work well in a quiz night: read the question, give teams a minute, then share the one-line explanation. The "why" is often what people remember.
