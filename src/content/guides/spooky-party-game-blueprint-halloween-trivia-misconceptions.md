**Most Halloween "facts" repeated at parties are half true: the holiday is old but its traditions changed, and a few famous stories were told wrong from the start.** These seven myths work as a quiz on their own. Read the myth aloud and ask whether it is true, then give the correction.

## Myth 1: Jack-o'-lanterns were always pumpkins

Before pumpkins, the lanterns were carved from root vegetables such as turnips, rutabagas and mangelwurzels, and the custom is tied to Ireland and Scotland. The carved pumpkin took over in the United States and Canada. The name "jack-o'-lantern" comes from the flickering lights seen over peat bogs.

## Myth 2: Halloween is a modern American invention

The name is short for All Hallows' Eve, the evening before All Saints' Day on November 1. The holiday is often linked to the Gaelic festival of Samhain, which marked the end of the harvest around the same date. How much of Samhain survives in modern Halloween is debated, so say "often linked" and not "descended from".

## Myth 3: Candy corn is a modern invention

It is more than a century old. George Renninger, who worked for the Wunderle Candy Company in Philadelphia, is credited with inventing it in the 1880s, and Wunderle made it from 1888. The Goelitz Confectionery Company, now Jelly Belly, began making it in 1898. It has also been sold as "Chicken Feed".

## Myth 4: The Salem witches were burned

No one at Salem was burned. Nineteen people were hanged, and one man, Giles Corey, died under torture for refusing to enter a plea. The idea that witches in America were burned is a popular but false addition made by later writers and film-makers.

## Myth 5: Bram Stoker wrote *Dracula* after visiting Transylvania

Stoker, an Irish writer, traveled widely as the manager of an actor's tours but never visited Eastern Europe, where his novel is set. The English town of Whitby plays a bigger role than most people remember: in the book, the ship *Demeter* arrives there.

## Myth 6: Frankenstein is the monster

Victor Frankenstein is the scientist who creates the creature; the creature has no name. Mary Shelley began the story in 1816 during a ghost-story contest with Lord Byron and others, and the novel was published anonymously in 1818.

## Myth 7: The Day of the Dead is "Mexican Halloween"

It is a separate tradition with its own meaning: a celebration of remembering friends and family who have died, with home altars and special foods, held on November 1 and 2. It overlaps with Halloween in date and in the shared roots of All Saints' and All Souls' observances, but it is not a version of it.

## A true-or-false round

Read each statement and ask true or false:

1. Jack-o'-lanterns were carved from turnips before pumpkins. **True.**
2. "Halloween" comes from All Hallows' Eve. **True.**
3. Candy corn was invented in the 1920s. **False**, in the 1880s.
4. Nineteen people were hanged at Salem. **True.**
5. Witches were burned at Salem. **False.**
6. Bram Stoker visited Transylvania before writing *Dracula*. **False.**
7. In *Dracula*, the ship *Demeter* arrives at Whitby. **True.**
8. Frankenstein is the name of the monster. **False**, it is the scientist.
9. *Frankenstein* was first published in 1818. **True.**
10. The Day of the Dead is held on November 1 and 2. **True.**

Eight points or more wins a prize. For a longer evening, add a costume round and read [how to host a trivia night](/guides/how-to-host-a-trivia-night/).
