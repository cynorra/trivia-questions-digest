Fifty-four countries make Africa the biggest single-continent test in geography, and most quiz scores leak away in the same five places. Several countries are small, and many borders were drawn in straight lines across deserts. So this guide starts with the traps, settles them one by one, and only then walks the five regions (North, West, Central, East and Southern Africa) that hold everything else.

## Five traps to settle first

**The two Congos.** The Democratic Republic of the Congo (DR Congo) is the huge one in the center of the continent, the second-largest country in Africa. The Republic of the Congo is much smaller and lies to its west. Their capitals, Kinshasa and Brazzaville, face each other across the Congo River.

**The three Guineas.** Guinea-Bissau is the small one on the coast north of Guinea; Equatorial Guinea is far away in Central Africa.

**Niger and Nigeria.** Niger is the large landlocked country in the Sahara. Nigeria is south of it on the coast, and it is the most populous country in Africa.

**Sudan and South Sudan.** South Sudan became independent in 2011 and is the smaller, southern one.

**Togo and Benin.** Two narrow countries side by side. Togo is the western one; Benin is the eastern one, next to Nigeria.

## North Africa (7)

**Morocco**, **Algeria**, **Tunisia**, **Libya**, **Egypt** and **Sudan** run along the north and east, with **Mauritania** on the Atlantic side. Algeria is the largest country in Africa. Egypt is easy: its border with Libya and Sudan is made of straight lines, and the Nile runs through it from south to north.

## West Africa (15)

The hardest region, because 15 countries share the coast and the Sahel, several of them small and similar in shape. Work along the coast from northwest to southeast:

1. **Senegal**, with **The Gambia** as a thin strip almost entirely inside it.
2. **Guinea-Bissau** and **Guinea**.
3. **Sierra Leone** and **Liberia**.
4. **Côte d'Ivoire** and **Ghana**.
5. **Togo** and **Benin**.
6. **Nigeria**.

Inland are **Mali**, **Burkina Faso** and **Niger**, and offshore is the island nation of **Cabo Verde**.

## Central Africa (9)

Around the DR Congo and the Republic of the Congo are **Cameroon**, **Central African Republic**, **Chad**, **Gabon**, **Equatorial Guinea**, **Angola** to the south, and the island nation of **São Tomé and Príncipe**. Northern Chad reaches deep into the Sahara.

## East Africa (14)

From the Horn of Africa southward: **Eritrea**, **Djibouti**, **Ethiopia** and **Somalia**, then **South Sudan**, **Uganda**, **Kenya**, **Rwanda**, **Burundi** and **Tanzania**. Rwanda and Burundi are the small pair between DR Congo and Tanzania; Rwanda is the northern one.

Offshore in the Indian Ocean are **Madagascar**, **Comoros**, **Mauritius** and **Seychelles**.

## Southern Africa (9)

**South Africa** anchors the south. **Lesotho** sits entirely inside it, and **Eswatini** is nearly surrounded, also bordering **Mozambique**. To the north are **Namibia**, **Botswana**, **Zimbabwe**, **Zambia** and **Malawi**. Malawi is the long, thin country along Lake Malawi.

The regions follow common usage, similar to the groupings used by the United Nations. Some countries are placed differently by different sources; what matters for learning is a consistent grouping.

## Records worth knowing

Africa has more countries than any other continent, all 54 of them United Nations members. Algeria is the largest by area and Nigeria the most populous. The smallest is Seychelles, an island nation in the Indian Ocean, by both area and population; on the mainland, The Gambia is the smallest.
