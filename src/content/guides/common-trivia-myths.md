<div class="key-takeaway"><p><strong>Quick answer:</strong> Some of the most repeated "fun facts" are false: the Great Wall of China isn't visible to the naked eye from the Moon, goldfish don't have three-second memories, Napoleon wasn't unusually short, and Vikings didn't wear horned helmets. Each of these is a great true-or-false question, as long as the host knows the right answer.</p></div>

Every trivia writer eventually repeats a "fact" that isn't true. These are the ones we see most often in trivia lists and fact-of-the-day posts. We leave them out of our [fun facts](/fun-facts/) and question sets, or include them only to correct them.

## Space and science

**1. "The Great Wall of China is visible from the Moon."** False. The wall is long but only a few meters wide, and similar in color to the land around it. NASA notes that it's hard to see with the naked eye even from low Earth orbit, let alone from the Moon, about 384,000 km away.

**2. "Lightning never strikes the same place twice."** False. Tall structures are hit again and again; the Empire State Building is struck about 20 times a year, according to the National Weather Service.

**3. "Glass is a slow-moving liquid, which is why old windows are thicker at the bottom."** False. Glass is an amorphous solid. Old panes are uneven because of how they were made; glaziers often set the thicker edge at the bottom.

**4. "Mercury is the hottest planet because it's closest to the Sun."** False. Venus is hotter, because its thick atmosphere traps heat. This one appears in our [science trivia](/science-trivia/).

**5. "We only use 10 percent of our brains."** False. Brain imaging shows activity across virtually the whole brain over the course of a day.

## Animals

**6. "Goldfish have a three-second memory."** False. Experiments have trained goldfish to respond to sounds and lights and to remember them for months.

**7. "Bulls are angered by the color red."** False. Cattle are red-green colorblind; it's the movement of the cape that provokes the bull.

**8. "Bats are blind."** False. All bats can see, and many species see well. Many also use echolocation to hunt in the dark.

**9. "Ostriches bury their heads in the sand."** False. They lower their heads to turn their eggs in nests dug in the ground, which from a distance can look like burying.

## History

**10. "Napoleon was very short."** Not really. He was about 5 feet 7 inches (around 1.70 m), about average for a Frenchman of his time. The myth comes partly from the difference between French and English inches, and partly from British cartoons.

**11. "Vikings wore horned helmets."** False. No horned helmet has been found from a Viking-age warrior's grave. The image was popularized by 19th-century art and costume design, especially for Wagner's operas.

**12. "Columbus proved the Earth was round."** False. Educated Europeans had known the Earth was round since ancient Greece. The argument in 1492 was about how big the Earth was; Columbus underestimated it.

**13. "Medieval people thought the Earth was flat."** Also false, for the same reason. The flat-Earth Middle Ages is largely a 19th-century myth.

## Food and everyday life

**14. "Sugar makes children hyperactive."** Controlled studies have generally not found that sugar changes children's behavior, though parents who believe their child has had sugar tend to rate them as more hyperactive.

**15. "It takes seven years to digest swallowed gum."** False. Gum base isn't fully digested, but it passes through the digestive system like other indigestible material, in days, not years.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Great Wall of China visible from space?

Not to the naked eye from the Moon, and only with difficulty, if at all, from low Earth orbit. It's narrow and blends into the surrounding land.

### Do goldfish really have a three-second memory?

No. Studies have shown goldfish can learn and remember cues, such as a feeding signal, for months.

### Was Napoleon short?

No. At about 5 feet 7 inches he was roughly average height for a Frenchman of his time.

### Why do false facts spread so easily?

They're simple, surprising and easy to repeat, and they get copied from list to list without anyone checking the source.
