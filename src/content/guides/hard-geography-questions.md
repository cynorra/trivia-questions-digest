A geography question is hard when the obvious answer is wrong. These twenty questions are chosen for that reason. Try each one before reading the answer, and keep score: most people get fewer than half.

## Questions about where things are

**1. Which city is farther west: Reno, Nevada, or Los Angeles, California?**
Reno. California's coast curves southeast, so Los Angeles sits east of Reno even though it's on the Pacific.

**2. Which is farther north: Rome or New York City?**
Rome, slightly. Rome is at about 41.9° north and New York City at about 40.7°. Europe feels farther south because the Gulf Stream keeps it mild.

**3. If you travel due south from Detroit, which country do you reach first?**
Canada. Windsor, Ontario, lies directly south of downtown Detroit, across the Detroit River.

**4. Which US state is closest to Africa?**
Maine. Its eastern tip is the closest point in the US to Africa, slightly closer than Florida or the Carolinas.

**5. Which country has the most time zones, counting overseas territories?**
France, with 12 (13 counting its Antarctic claim), because of territories from the Caribbean to the Pacific. Russia has the most within one continuous landmass, with 11.

## Questions about size

**6. Which is larger by area: Greenland or Africa?**
Africa, by about fourteen times. On the common Mercator map Greenland looks similar in size, because that projection stretches land near the poles.

**7. Which country has the longest coastline?**
Canada. Its Arctic islands give it more coastline than any other country.

**8. Which is the largest country entirely in Europe?**
Ukraine. Russia is larger but spans Europe and Asia.

**9. Which US state has the longest coastline?**
Alaska, longer than all the other states combined.

**10. Is Australia an island?**
It's usually called a continent rather than an island. That's why Greenland is listed as the world's largest island.

## Questions about capitals

**11. What is the capital of Australia?**
Canberra. Sydney and Melbourne were rivals, and Canberra was built as a compromise between them.

**12. What is the capital of Canada?**
Ottawa, not Toronto.

**13. What is the capital of Turkey?**
Ankara, not Istanbul.

**14. Which country has three capital cities?**
South Africa: Pretoria (executive), Cape Town (legislative) and Bloemfontein (judicial).

**15. What is the capital of Myanmar?**
Naypyidaw, which replaced Yangon in the mid-2000s.

## Questions about water

**16. Which river is longer, the Nile or the Amazon?**
Most references say the Nile, but some measurements put the Amazon ahead. The answer depends on where each river's source is placed.

**17. What is the largest lake in the world?**
The Caspian Sea, which despite its name is a lake. The largest freshwater lake by area is Lake Superior.

**18. Which ocean is the smallest?**
The Arctic Ocean.

## Questions about borders

**19. Which two countries share the longest border?**
Canada and the United States.

**20. Which three countries are completely surrounded by a single other country?**
Lesotho, inside South Africa, and San Marino and Vatican City, both inside Italy.

## Why these questions are hard

Most of these trip people up for one of three reasons:

- **Map distortion.** The Mercator projection, used on many wall maps and online maps, makes places near the poles look huge. That's why Greenland seems as big as Africa and why Europe seems farther north than it is.
- **Famous-city bias.** We assume the biggest or best-known city is the capital: Sydney, Toronto, Istanbul.
- **Mental maps that run straight north–south.** We picture the US West Coast as a vertical line, so Reno being west of Los Angeles feels wrong.

The [world map quiz](/world-map-quiz/) uses the Equal Earth projection, which keeps countries' relative sizes accurate, so practicing on it slowly corrects the Mercator habit.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the hardest geography question?

There's no single hardest question, but those built on map distortion, such as comparing Greenland and Africa, are missed most often.

### How can I get better at hard geography questions?

Practice on blank maps, learn the capitals that aren't the largest city, and pay attention to map projections. Each habit removes a common trap.

### Are these questions good for a trivia night?

Yes. Read the question, give teams a minute, then share the one-line explanation; the "why" is often what people remember.
