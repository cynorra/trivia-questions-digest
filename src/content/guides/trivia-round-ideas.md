Six straight rounds of spoken questions can feel long by round four. Changing the format, not just the subject, is what keeps a room awake. Here are 25 round ideas, grouped by type, with a note on how to run each one.

## Subject rounds

These are the backbone of any quiz. Pull ready-made questions from our category pages.

1. **General knowledge.** The classic opener. Use [general knowledge trivia](/general-knowledge-trivia/).
2. **History.** US presidents, wars and famous firsts. Try [history trivia](/history-trivia/).
3. **Science and nature.** Space, animals and the human body: [science trivia](/science-trivia/).
4. **Sports.** Stick to rules and history so answers don't go out of date: [sports trivia](/sports-trivia/).
5. **Movies and TV.** Famous lines and Oscar history: [movie trivia](/movie-trivia/).
6. **Music.** Bands, albums and composers: [music trivia](/music-trivia/).
7. **Food and drink.** Where dishes come from: [food trivia](/food-trivia/).
8. **Bible.** For church groups, with verse references: [Bible trivia](/bible-trivia/).
9. **Decades.** All questions from one decade, such as the 1960s. Our [trivia for seniors](/trivia-for-seniors/) has a good 1950s–70s set.
10. **Local trivia.** Your town, your office, your school. Nobody else's quiz can have this round.

## Format rounds

11. **Picture round.** Hand out a sheet of 10 to 20 images at the start: famous faces, logos, landmarks or zoomed-in objects. Teams work on it all night and hand it in at the break.
12. **Music intros.** Play the first five seconds of a song; teams name the artist and title. Two points per song, one for each.
13. **True or false.** Fast and easy to mark. Use our [fun facts](/fun-facts/) for true statements and the myths in [common trivia myths](/guides/common-trivia-myths/) for false ones.
14. **Connections.** Ten questions whose answers share a hidden link; a bonus point for the team that names the link.
15. **Closest number.** Every answer is a number, and the closest team gets the point. Good as a final round.
16. **Missing word.** Read a famous line, title or quote with a word left out.
17. **Odd one out.** Four items; teams pick the one that doesn't belong and say why.
18. **First letter.** Every answer in the round starts with the same letter.
19. **Anagrams.** Scrambled names of countries, films or bands. Hand out on paper.
20. **Put in order.** Four events or inventions, ordered by date. Full marks for the exact order only.

## Theme and seasonal rounds

21. **Christmas.** Songs, films and traditions: [Christmas trivia](/christmas-trivia/).
22. **Thanksgiving.** The 1621 feast and the holiday's history: [Thanksgiving trivia](/thanksgiving-trivia/).
23. **Halloween.** Horror classics and customs: [Halloween trivia](/halloween-trivia/).
24. **Costume round.** At a Halloween party, each team describes a famous character in three clues; the others guess.
25. **This day in history.** Questions about events that happened on the date of your quiz.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are good trivia categories?

General knowledge, history, science, sports, movies and TV, music and food are the most popular. Add one format round, such as pictures or music intros, and one seasonal theme.

### How many rounds should a pub quiz have?

Six rounds of ten questions is the usual pattern. Four rounds is enough for a family gathering or a short office game.

### What is a picture round?

A sheet of images, such as famous faces or logos, handed out at the start of the night. Teams identify as many as they can and hand the sheet in at the break.

### What's a fun trivia round for work?

True or false and closest-number rounds work well at work: they're fast, need no special knowledge, and the answers start conversations.
