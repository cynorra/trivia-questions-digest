Knowing where the 50 states are is one of those skills everyone assumes they have until they face a blank map. The map has a structure, and once you see it, memorizing the states stops being a list of 50 names and becomes four small puzzles: nine, twelve, sixteen and thirteen states, each of which you can hold in your head in one sitting. Fifty you cannot. Most people can place all 50 within a week of short daily sessions, so this guide is laid out day by day. It works for students studying for a geography test and for adults who simply want to stop mixing up Iowa and Idaho.

The four puzzles are the US Census Bureau's regions, a convenient way to cut the map into pieces of manageable size.

## Day 1: the Northeast (9 states, 10 minutes)

Maine, New Hampshire, Vermont, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, Connecticut, New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania.

**Anchor:** New York, the largest state in the region, with New England fanning out to its east. **Watch:** Vermont and New Hampshire are the classic mirror pair: Vermont is wider at the top, New Hampshire wider at the bottom. Rhode Island and Connecticut are small, so zoom in on a phone.

Finish with the [Northeast states quiz](/northeast-states-quiz/) until you get all 9.

## Day 2: the West (13 states, 15 minutes)

Montana, Idaho, Wyoming, Colorado, New Mexico, Arizona, Utah, Nevada, Washington, Oregon, California, Alaska, Hawaii.

**Anchor:** California and its long coast, then Nevada inland beside it. Wyoming and Colorado are both rectangles; Wyoming is the northern one. Utah, Colorado, Arizona and New Mexico meet at the Four Corners, the only place in the country where four states touch. Idaho has a panhandle reaching up to Canada.

Quiz yourself with the [Western states quiz](/western-states-quiz/), then retake the Northeast once.

## Day 3: the Midwest (12 states, 15 minutes)

Ohio, Indiana, Illinois, Michigan, Wisconsin, Minnesota, Iowa, Missouri, North Dakota, South Dakota, Nebraska, Kansas.

**Anchor:** Michigan, the only state made of two peninsulas, with the Great Lakes around it. The plains stack runs from north to south: North Dakota, South Dakota, Nebraska and Kansas sit in a near-perfect column, with Oklahoma and Texas below. Iowa sits between the Mississippi and Missouri rivers.

Quiz with the [Midwest states quiz](/midwest-states-quiz/).

## Day 4: the South (16 states, 15 minutes)

Delaware, Maryland, Virginia, West Virginia, North Carolina, South Carolina, Georgia, Florida, Kentucky, Tennessee, Alabama, Mississippi, Arkansas, Louisiana, Oklahoma, Texas.

**Anchor:** Texas, the largest of the lower 48, with Florida's peninsula anchoring the other end. Remember the pairs: North Carolina above South Carolina, and West Virginia lies northwest of Virginia, not west of it. Alabama is the eastern one of Alabama and Mississippi. The Mississippi River forms much of the border between the states on either side of it, from Minnesota and Wisconsin down to Louisiana and Mississippi.

Quiz with the [Southern states quiz](/southern-states-quiz/). This is the biggest group, so take it slowly.

## Day 5: all 50 together (10 minutes)

Take the [full map quiz](/us-states-map-quiz/). Each quiz gives you three clicks per state; if you miss three times, the map highlights the right state and adds it to a "practice these" list at the end. That list is your homework for the next session.

## Day 6: only the misses (10 minutes)

Work through the states on your "practice these" list, then play the full map again.

## Day 7: the test (5 minutes)

Play the full map with no study first, and aim for 50 on the first try. Short sessions spread over several days work better than one long session, because each day you retrieve what you learned the day before. That act of remembering is what makes it stick.

## The states people miss most

- **Rhode Island, Delaware and Connecticut**, simply because they're small.
- **Iowa and Idaho.** Iowa is Midwest; Idaho is West, with the panhandle.
- **Alabama and Mississippi.** Alabama is the eastern one.
- **Vermont and New Hampshire.** Use the mirror trick from Day 1.
- **Alaska and Hawaii**, which most US maps, including ours, show as insets in the lower left so the map can show all 50 states at a readable size. Alaska is far larger than it looks there, and their real positions are far to the northwest and southwest.

## After the map: capitals

Once you can place the states, the next step for most people is their capitals. The [state capitals quiz](/state-capitals-quiz/) works the same way, region by region, and the full list of states and capitals is on that page.
